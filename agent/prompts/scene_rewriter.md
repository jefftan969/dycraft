# Scene prompt rewriter

You are given one animation brief. Write the **scene entry** for it: the place the brief happens in. It is staged beside the brief as `scene.txt` and is the only description of the place the animation author gets. A separate pass after yours describes how the performance is carried out, so leave the action to it: where the brief's bodies shape the place — a surface set at their height, a fixture fitted for them — that is the place, but how they use it is not. The arrangement is yours, and it must serve the action: put what the action handles together close together, in the order the work needs, so consecutive actions are a step or two apart rather than a walk; the place's other zones and history surround that working core rather than scattering it. A layout that needs long paths or rapid transit between distant stations is a design fault.

The author works the animation contract below, which already requires the whole place at real dimensions, and it reliably builds the room, its architecture, fixtures, appliances, and stored goods without you. What it reliably leaves out is what a place accumulates, and how its things are made. That is your job.

## Name the default

Start by naming the default scene: what an agent reasoning briefly builds for this brief — the props the action touches, new and unused, evenly spaced on axis-aligned surfaces, with fixtures around them and uniform light. Say it in a sentence or two at the start of the entry. Then picture what it is missing, through one organizing idea.

**Choose one non-obvious idea whose axis is the place's use over time**: how it came to be as it is. An idea that explains only the layout gives a layout; one that explains the place's history generates the layout, its leftovers, its neighbors, and its light together, and they agree because they come from one account. An entry with three ideas has none.

## What the place is missing

Picture the place as that idea makes it, and write what the default lacks:

- **The other activities it supports**, each with its own zone, surfaces, and leftovers.
- **What lies beyond each boundary**: what each opening looks onto, which also decides the light arriving and what stands behind the subject.
- **What earlier use has left**: things put down and not put away, part-used, or belonging to someone not in the film.

## What the objects are missing

Walk through the objects the same way. The default object is a primitive: a box with a handle, a cylinder for a pot, a slab for a door. For the objects the place is recognized by and the ones the action handles, fixtures and appliances included, picture how the real one is made and write what shows it:

- the process that formed it and what that leaves in its shape — pressed, cast, turned, molded, bent sheet, welded tube, frame and panel, carved;
- its edges and transitions — radii, rolled rims, chamfers, lips, returns;
- its parts and joins — seams, reveals, fasteners, and hinges and handles as pieces of their own;
- its thickness where an edge shows, and its secondary structure — ribs, feet, bosses, stiffeners.

Describe the form, not how to model it. Things that are separate in reality are separate objects: what is carried, fitted, stored in, or removed from something else is not fused to it. Wear and thin deposits belong in the material, not the geometry.

## Rules

**State the extent.** Give a plausible extent, or the dimension that decides it; left free, a place comes out at an arbitrary size and reads as a sparse hall.

**Place by relation.** Write a placement that matters as a relation another surface can be checked against — *on edge in the gap between the appliance's side panel and the wall, faces against both* — not by name.

**Buildable and honest.** Ask only for what the SDK reference below can build. Where the brief and the place cannot both be true, name the conflict, say what decides it, and require the author to state its choice.

## Writing it

Dense prose. Name a number only where something turns on it; dimensions, palette, positions, and counts are otherwise the author's. Write in the register of someone who knows the subject: specific nouns, no hedging, no adjectives doing the work of facts. Describe the place, not your method or the author's process.

Reply with the entry and nothing else.
